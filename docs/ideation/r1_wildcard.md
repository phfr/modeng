**Framing of this lens.** The label we predict is not "gene is in the module". It is "a public database attached evidence for this
gene to this disease id between two releases". That is a *detection event* produced by instruments (GWAS of growing size, L2G,
ClinVar submissions, curators, ontology mapping). Physics and survey astronomy separate the source population from the selection
function of the instrument; epidemiology separates incidence from ascertainment. The transfer model does neither: it learns the product.
Most ideas below model the instrument explicitly, because the brief says the hard strata are instrument-driven: 57 % of new positives
are GWAS-only and 82 % of strictly-new ones are (brief 4); ~40 % of "new" positives are old evidence newly attached (brief 5).

## Step 1. Eight assumptions of transfer LightGBM and the baselines, and what follows if each is false

| # | Assumption | If false |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **The label is the biology.** A new association means a gene joined the module. | The label is biology times detection. Then the instrument (study size, L2G, curation, id mapping) is a separate, predictable factor, and modelling it directly should reach exactly the strata where biology-similarity fails (GWAS-only, re-attached old evidence; brief 4, 5). |
| A2 | **Unlabelled = uniformly unknown.** Every non-seed gene is the same kind of negative. | Some unlabelled genes are measured nulls (well-powered GWAS z near 0), others were never observable (no SNP coverage: ~2,250 genes have no MAGMA test in the file I read). Upper limits should demote the first group; missing mass should be reserved for the second. |
| A3 | **Stationarity.** The process that made the 21.04 labels is the one that makes the 21.04 to 25.09 arrivals. | Arrivals come from a different channel mix than the stock (82 % of strictly-new are GWAS-only, brief 4). A model trained on the stock ranks stock-like genes. One must train on *flows* and forecast the channel mix per disease. |
| A4 | **Knowledge is a static snapshot.** Only the T0 matrix matters, not how it got there. | Evidence has momentum: genes and disease areas whose evidence is accelerating at T0 are the next arrivals. The 19.04 to 21.04 flux and evidence dates carry information that the snapshot lacks. |
| A5 | **Disease identity is the ontology id.** Similarity between diseases is seed-set overlap between ids. | The id space lags the source label space. Evidence for a gene may already exist at T0 under a free-text source label or another id that later maps to the target (brief 5: identifier migration is a sizable share). Similarity should be computed in raw source-label space. |
| A6 | **Pairwise co-association is the right statistic** (`tr_max`: max over donors of a marginal overlap score). | Marginal co-association mixes direct and transitive coupling, as in protein contact prediction before direct-coupling analysis. A global model (inverse Ising / max-entropy) would separate them and combine many weak donors coherently instead of taking a max. |
| A7 | **The module has no size.** The model outputs a ranking; the cutoff is external. Every disease gets the same exploit/explore allocation. | Diseases differ in completeness. A saturated module (few singletons, high source overlap) should rank only high-support genes; an early-stage module should spend rank budget on low-support genes. Completeness is estimable from the evidence multiplicity spectrum. |
| A8 | **All information is in biology or genetics databases.** | Informed actors (funders, companies, consortia) move before public evidence appears. Grant, patent and study-registration records are dated leading indicators that no biological feature contains. |

## Step 2. Ideas

Baseline reference for all "expected effect" lines: transfer LightGBM `modet-U-cov-Lb`, nuAP 0.177 dev / 0.168 devall on the temporal STRING
panel; `tr_max` alone 0.122; minimum detectable effect ~0.01 nuAP (brief 1, 7). All expected effects are my estimates, not measurements.

### WC-L2GEMU: Emulate the label generator: locus crossing times host-gene prior
- **Mechanism.** A GWAS-only arrival is the output of a pipeline: a locus crosses genome-wide significance in a larger study, then L2G picks a gene in it. Score gene g for disease d as P(a locus near g crosses at the forecast sample size | T0 sub-threshold SNP statistics) x P(L2G picks g | locus), with the second factor learned from T0 `ot_genetics_portal` rows (which gene in a locus received the top L2G, as a function of distance rank, coding variant, gene density, constraint). This is the gravitational-wave follow-up recipe: sky localisation probability x host-galaxy prior x detection probability (Nuttall and Sutton report a factor 2-4 gain from adding host weighting to the error box alone).
- **p_typical** 0.15 (sub-threshold GWAS is obvious from brief 4; modelling the L2G labeler as the second factor is not).
- **Data.** On disk: `gwas_magma_t0/*_leads.parquet` and `*_w0/_w10.parquet` (108 T0 studies), `cs2g_lead_links.parquet`, `gwas_sumstats_t0` (90 GB) for SNP-level sub-threshold peaks, `evidence_2104/sourceId=ot_genetics_portal` (L2G training pairs, with `studySampleSize`). Leakage class: **temporal-clean** if only T0 studies and 21.04 L2G rows are used. Main leakage route: a T0-dated GCST whose summary statistics were re-deposited or updated after 2021-04, and the post-T0 `gwas_magma/` directory being picked up by mistake.
- **Minimal test (<= 2 h, CPU).** (1) From `evidence_2104` L2G rows build locus groups per study (genes sharing a `studyId` and lead variant), train a small LightGBM "which gene wins" on diseases outside dev (10 min). (2) For dev temporal diseases with a T0 study, take sub-threshold peaks (1e-8 < P < 1e-4) from the `_leads` files or a clumped pass over the sumstats, compute crossing probability at N' = 2N with a winner's-curse-shrunk z (z' ~ z_shrunk * sqrt(N'/N)), spread it over genes within 500 kb with the host model (30-60 min). (3) `score = max(rank-normalised transfer, w * l2gemu)` with w chosen on non-dev diseases; evaluate `temporal` dev, all targets and GWAS-only targets separately (`fy_gwas` in the arrival file). Sketch: `S = wc_l2gemu.score(tasks, B); metrics.evaluate(S, tasks)`.
- **Expected effect.** On dev diseases that have a T0 study: recall@100 of GWAS-only arrivals from 16 % (brief 4) to 25-30 %; nuAP +0.02 to +0.04 on that subset, +0.01 to +0.015 over all dev diseases (coverage-limited); AP +0.01. Reason: sub-threshold own-trait z already has AUC ~0.8 (brief 4), and the host factor sharpens gene choice inside a locus where MAGMA spreads signal over neighbours. **Kill rule:** GWAS-only recall@100 gain < 4 points or nuAP gain < 0.01 on the covered subset.
- **Most likely failure.** Coverage: too few dev diseases have a T0 study with summary statistics, so the pooled effect is below the MDE even if the per-disease effect is real.

### WC-XPOWER: Crossing forecast for diseases without their own GWAS
- **Mechanism.** For a disease with no T0 summary statistics, build a proxy gene-z as a genetic-correlation-weighted combination of the 108 T0 trait z-vectors (weights from seed-gene z enrichment, regularised toward the Neale rg table where a UKB proxy trait exists), then convert to a *crossing probability* P(z' > threshold at N') using an empirical-Bayes effect-size prior per trait, not a linear feature. The crossing transform is strongly non-linear: it ignores genes already significant (they are seeds or excluded) and genes far from threshold, and concentrates mass on the band that a 2-4x larger study will reveal. Per-disease N' is forecast from the trajectory of `studySampleSize` over `publicationYear` in the T0 L2G rows of the disease area.
- **p_typical** 0.35. Banned-list note: "T0 UKB MAGMA studies as donors" was tried as donors in the transfer matrix. The new reason here is the detection non-linearity plus the sample-size forecast: the earlier use asked "does this gene belong to a related trait" (already answered by transfer), this asks "is this gene in the band about to cross". Pleiotropy-informed conditional FDR shows that conditioning on a correlated trait raises replication rate of sub-threshold loci (Andreassen 2013).
- **Data.** On disk: `gwas_magma_t0/` (4.4 GB), `neale_rg_table/`, `evidence_2104/sourceId=ot_genetics_portal`. **Temporal-clean.** Main leakage route: choosing the trait weights with any post-T0 information (must use T0 seeds only), and the rg table release date (files are dated 2019-2022; restrict to the 2019 h2/rg results).
- **Minimal test (<= 1 h, CPU).** Load 108 `_w10` z-vectors into a [108, n_genes] matrix; per dev task fit non-negative ridge weights of seed indicator on trait z (leave seeds out of the scored genes); per trait fit a two-component normal mixture to z (ashr-like, seconds); crossing prob at N' = 3N; proxy score = sum_k w_k * crossprob_k. Compare three arms on `temporal` dev: linear proxy z, crossing proxy, crossing proxy + transfer. `S = wc_xpower.score(tasks, B)`.
- **Expected effect.** nuAP +0.005 to +0.012 over transfer LightGBM, concentrated on zero-transfer-support GWAS-only positives (top-100 recall there from 0.4 % (brief 3) to 3-5 %); AP ~+0.003. The crossing arm should beat the linear arm by >= 30 % relative on GWAS-only recall, otherwise the non-linearity claim is false. **Kill rule:** combined nuAP gain < 0.005, or crossing arm not better than the linear arm.
- **Most likely failure.** The 108 T0 traits do not span the dev diseases (few diseases have a correlated trait with enough power), so the proxy is noise for most tasks.

### WC-BACKLOG: Forecast the curation backlog in raw source-label space
- **Mechanism.** About 40 % of "new" positives are old evidence (dated <= 2020) newly attached, and identifier migration is a sizable share (brief 5). At T0 that evidence already sits in `evidence_2104` under a free-text `diseaseFromSource` label and a source id (MedGen CUI, RCV accession) mapped to some *other* disease id. Score gene g for disease d by the best match between d (name, synonyms, cross-references from the 21.04 ontology) and the source labels/CUIs of g's T0 evidence rows that are not mapped to d or its family: exact CUI/xref match, then text-embedding similarity. This is transfer through the label space the ontology has not caught up with, and it needs no shared genes, so it works for rare diseases with 3-5 seeds.
- **p_typical** 0.05. Banned-list note: "identifier-repair label audit" proposed removing these arrivals as artefacts. The new reason: they are predictable, they are in the benchmark as scored positives, and for a user the statement "this gene already has ClinVar evidence under a label that means your disease" is a correct and useful module member.
- **Data.** On disk: `evidence_2104/sourceId={eva,eva_somatic,uniprot_variants,uniprot_literature,gene2phenotype,genomics_england,clingen,europepmc}` (inside the 4.0 GB), `hpo_annotations_2021-01_wayback`, `ot2104` disease index (names, synonyms, xrefs). Optional: a sentence-embedding model (local, CPU is enough for ~50k distinct labels). **Temporal-clean** (all inputs are the 21.04 release). Main leakage route: using a current ontology (EFO/MONDO 2025) for synonyms or xrefs, which contains the later mapping itself; an embedding model trained after 2021 carries diffuse knowledge, so report the exact-match arm separately.
- **Minimal test (<= 1.5 h, CPU).** (1) Read the eight sources with columns `targetId, diseaseId, diseaseFromSource, diseaseFromSourceId, diseaseFromSourceMappedId` (~10 min). (2) Arm A: exact string/CUI/xref match of source label to the task disease's 21.04 names/synonyms/xrefs where `diseaseId` is not in the task's family rows. Arm B: cosine similarity of label embeddings, max over the gene's rows. (3) Evaluate on `temporal` dev, overall and split by arrival class (`fy_clinvar`, `fy_curated`, `id_unmapped`, `persistent` in the arrival file) and on the strict endpoint `sa` (tasks.py). `S = wc_backlog.score(tasks, B)`.
- **Expected effect.** AP +0.02 to +0.04 and nuAP +0.015 to +0.03 over transfer LightGBM on all targets, because the addressable share is up to 40 % of positives and many are non-hub Mendelian genes that nuAP rewards. On the strict endpoint `sa` I expect about +0.005: that endpoint is built to exclude this class. **Kill rule:** nuAP gain < 0.01 on all targets, or arm A finds < 5 % of the old-evidence arrivals in the top 100.
- **Most likely failure.** The re-attached evidence was not in the 21.04 evidence dump under any label (it entered through a source OT added later), so there is nothing at T0 to match. Second risk: transfer already ranks these genes well because sibling diseases carry them.

### WC-SELFN: Survey selection function: channel-mixture arrival model
- **Mechanism.** Write arrival probability as a sum over evidence channels m (GWAS/L2G, ClinVar, gene panels, somatic, literature, animal model): P(g arrives in d) = sum_m pi(m | d) * P(g | d, m), where pi(m | d) is the forecast channel mix of the disease (from its T0 evidence composition and its 19.04 to 21.04 flux) and each channel has its own gene model with its own observability terms (GWAS: SNP coverage `nsnps`, gene length, LD; ClinVar: constraint, panel membership; somatic: mutational burden). Each channel model is trained on *flows* of that channel in non-dev diseases, not on the stock. This is the "adjacent possible" view: a gene becomes discoverable for a disease when a channel active in that disease can see that gene.
- **p_typical** 0.07. Banned-list note: "arrival-class decomposition" was a planning item for stratified evaluation; "tier-0 gene-propensity prior" was one disease-independent prior. This is a generative mixture whose weights are disease-specific and forecast, used for scoring.
- **Data.** On disk: `evidence_2104/` (per-source rows for channel stocks), `groundtruth/ot_t19_sets.parquet` and `ot_t0_sets.parquet` (flux), `gwas_magma_t0` (`nsnps`), `gnomad_v2.1.1`, `covariates/`. **Temporal-clean.** Main leakage route: learning the channel priors from the 21.04 to 25.09 arrival table of dev diseases (the arrival file exists on disk and must only be used for evaluation or for non-dev training diseases).
- **Minimal test (<= 2 h, CPU).** Build per-channel 19.04 to 21.04 flow labels from `evidence_2104` minus the 19.04 sets (channel assignment by `datasourceId`); train one LightGBM per channel with transfer + channel-specific covariates on non-dev diseases; pi(m | d) = smoothed share of channel m in d's 19.04 to 21.04 flux (fallback: therapeutic-area mean). Score dev tasks with the mixture; compare with a single pooled flow model and with the stock-trained transfer model. `S = wc_selfn.score(tasks, B)`.
- **Expected effect.** nuAP +0.008 to +0.015, AP +0.005; the gain should come from GWAS-only recall (16 % to ~22 %) because the GWAS channel model is no longer diluted by Mendelian-stock patterns. **Kill rule:** mixture not better than the pooled flow model by >= 0.005 nuAP (then the channel structure adds nothing over training on flows).
- **Most likely failure.** The 19.04 to 21.04 flux per channel is too small or too dominated by pipeline changes between those two releases to train on; the channel mix of 2021-2025 is not forecastable from it.

### WC-HAWKES: Self-exciting evidence flux and transfer velocity
- **Mechanism.** Treat evidence items as events of a mutually exciting point process over (gene, disease-area) cells: an event in a neighbouring disease or for the same gene raises the short-term rate in the target cell, with a decay kernel. Practical form: compute transfer support at 19.04 and at 21.04 and use the *increment* (velocity) and its second difference, plus dated event counts per gene and per disease family (`publicationYear` in L2G rows; PMID years for literature rows) convolved with an exponential kernel fitted on non-dev diseases. A gene with low but rapidly rising support is ranked above a gene with the same level and flat history.
- **p_typical** 0.12.
- **Data.** On disk: `groundtruth/ot_t19_sets.parquet` (3,435 diseases), `ot_t0_sets.parquet`, `evidence_2104/` (dates for the genetics source; `literature` PMIDs for europepmc). PMID-to-year: PubMed baseline or Europe PMC bulk metadata (download, a few GB; only years <= 2021-04 used). **Temporal-clean.** Main leakage route: the 19.04 sets were rebuilt with a later id mapping, so "velocity" partly encodes mapping changes; and PMID years from a current dump must be filtered by date.
- **Minimal test (<= 1 h, CPU, no download).** Compute `tr_max` on the 19.04 context and the 21.04 context (same code path as the transfer baseline, 19.04 universe mapped to the 21.04 ids); features: level, delta, delta of donor count, gene-level delta of number of diseases. Fit a LightGBM on non-dev tasks with and without the delta features; evaluate `temporal` dev. `S = wc_hawkes.score(tasks, B)`.
- **Expected effect.** nuAP +0.004 to +0.010, AP +0.005; the effect should sit in the low-support stratum (support present but weak), not in zero-support genes. **Kill rule:** < 0.004 nuAP over the same model without velocity features.
- **Most likely failure.** Two snapshots give one difference, which is dominated by OT pipeline changes between 19.04 and 21.04 (scoring and data-source changes), not by real discovery momentum.

### WC-CAPTURE: Capture-recapture completeness and a data-driven explore budget
- **Mechanism.** Treat evidence sources as independent capture occasions and estimate, per disease, how much of the module is still unseen: Good-Toulmin on the multiplicity spectrum (number of genes supported by 1, 2, 3... independent evidence items or sources) and log-linear capture-recapture across sources. The estimate gives (a) a calibrated module size and cutoff, which the brief asks for and no current method outputs, and (b) a per-disease mixing weight between an exploit ranker (transfer) and an explore ranker (any zero-support model such as WC-L2GEMU or WC-XPOWER): saturated diseases get no explore slots, early-stage diseases get many.
- **p_typical** 0.03. Banned-list note: "reserving top-100 slots for propagation" used a fixed quota; here the quota is estimated per disease from data and can be zero.
- **Data.** On disk: `evidence_2104/` (per-source rows, `studyId`/`literature` for item multiplicity), 21.04 sets. **Temporal-clean.** Main leakage route: none for the estimator itself; the mixing function must be fitted on non-dev diseases only.
- **Minimal test (<= 1 h, CPU).** Per disease: f1, f2 (genes with exactly one / two independent evidence items at T0), Chao1 and Good-Toulmin extrapolation at t = 1; number of sources per gene for a two-source Lincoln-Petersen estimate. Test 1 (no model needed): Spearman correlation across temporal diseases between predicted unseen count and the observed number of arrivals (`n_new` in `ot_temporal.parquet`), against the naive predictor `n_t0`. Test 2: interleave transfer LightGBM with the `degree`/`rwr` baseline or WC-XPOWER using weight = predicted missing-mass fraction; compare with a fixed weight.
- **Expected effect.** Test 1: Spearman >= 0.5 and at least 0.1 above the `n_t0` predictor. Test 2: nuAP +0.003 to +0.006 over the fixed-weight blend, AP unchanged; the main product is the calibrated module size, measured as error of predicted versus realised arrival count. **Kill rule:** Spearman gain over `n_t0` < 0.05 and blend gain < 0.003.
- **Most likely failure.** Evidence sources are not independent captures (ClinVar, G2P and panels copy each other), so the estimator measures curation redundancy, and arrival counts are driven by external study size, not by module completeness.

### WC-DCA: Inverse-Ising direct couplings between genes, amortised to unseen genes
- **Mechanism.** Treat each disease's gene set as one sample of a binary spin configuration and fit a pairwise maximum-entropy model (pseudo-likelihood inverse Ising with strong sparsity), as direct-coupling analysis does for residue contacts: couplings J_ij separate direct co-membership from transitive correlation through hub diseases. Score = conditional field on gene g given the seeds clamped on. Second stage: regress J_ij on pair features (ESM C similarity, co-essentiality, shared complex) so that couplings can be predicted for genes with no disease history, which is the only route by which this idea reaches zero-support genes.
- **p_typical** 0.06.
- **Data.** On disk: 21.04 context matrix (2,958 diseases x 19,255 genes via `B.context_for(task)`), `features/` (ESM C 300M), subex DepMap 20Q4 derived matrices, CORUM. **Temporal-clean** on `temporal` (DepMap 20Q4, CORUM v3.0, ESM C trained on sequence only). Main leakage route: fitting J on rows that include the task's family (must use the masked context), which with ~3k samples means refitting or an exact leave-family-out correction.
- **Minimal test (<= 2 h, CPU; GPU optional through `gpu-run`, not required).** Restrict to genes with >= 3 diseases at T0; L1-regularised logistic pseudo-likelihood per gene on the disease x gene matrix with family rows dropped by therapeutic-area fold (32 cores, ~30-60 min with a top-500 correlated-gene pre-filter per gene); score = J[:, seeds].sum(1) + h. Compare with `tr_max` and with item-item cosine on `temporal` dev and on `holdout` dev. Stage 2 only if stage 1 passes.
- **Expected effect.** Stage 1: nuAP +0.005 to +0.01 over `tr_max` alone (0.122), likely +0.002 to +0.005 on top of the LightGBM; larger on `holdout`. Stage 2: within-pool AUC for genes unknown to any disease from ~0.60-0.63 (brief 3) to ~0.66. **Kill rule:** stage 1 < +0.005 nuAP over `tr_max` alone on temporal dev.
- **Most likely failure.** ~3k samples for ~10k spins is far below what inverse Ising needs; with the regularisation required, J collapses to the same co-occurrence statistic that transfer already uses.

### WC-UPPERLIM: Upper limits: measured nulls as negative evidence
- **Mechanism.** Astronomy catalogues report non-detections with upper limits, and they constrain models as much as detections. For a disease with a T0 GWAS, a gene with many tested SNPs and z near 0 has a bounded effect and a near-zero probability of becoming a GWAS-only arrival at 2-4x sample size; current methods treat it like any other unlabelled gene. Multiply the transfer score's GWAS-channel component by the crossing probability (a penalty for well-powered nulls, neutral for untested genes), and move the freed top-100 slots to genes that are untested or sub-threshold.
- **p_typical** 0.08.
- **Data.** On disk: `gwas_magma_t0/*_w10.parquet` (`z`, `nsnps`, `n`). **Temporal-clean.** Main leakage route: none beyond the study-date check of WC-L2GEMU.
- **Minimal test (<= 30 min, CPU).** For dev temporal diseases with a T0 study: among the top-200 genes of the transfer baseline, compare the realised positive rate of genes with (nsnps >= 50, |z| < 0.5) against the rest; then rescore `S' = S * (a + (1 - a) * crossprob)` with a fitted on non-dev diseases and evaluate. No training needed.
- **Expected effect.** Precision in the top 100 rises by removing 5-10 measured-null hubs per disease: AP +0.003 to +0.008, nuAP +0.003 to +0.006 on covered diseases. **Kill rule:** positive rate of measured-null genes in the transfer top-200 is not at least 30 % lower than that of the rest (then nulls carry no information and the idea is dead before any rescoring).
- **Most likely failure.** Many arrivals are not GWAS-driven (Mendelian, somatic), and common-variant nulls say nothing about them; the penalty then removes true positives. Mostafavi et al. 2023 show that constrained genes are systematically depleted of common-variant signal, which is this failure in mechanism form.

### WC-MARKET: Revealed preferences of funders and filers as leading indicators
- **Mechanism.** Grants, patents and study registrations are dated bets by informed actors and precede public evidence by years. Build a dated gene x disease co-mention flux from NIH project abstracts (fiscal year <= 2020) and gene-disease patent annotations (filing date <= 2021-04), normalise it by the gene's and the disease's overall attention (so that it measures a specific bet, not popularity; Stoeger et al. 2018 show overall attention is predictable from a few gene properties and is self-reinforcing), and use the residual as an arrival signal. It is a non-biological information source that can exist for a gene with no association to any disease.
- **p_typical** 0.05.
- **Data.** Download: NIH ExPORTER project and abstract files FY1985-2020 (https://reporter.nih.gov/exporter, I estimate a few GB; size not verified today); optional SureChEMBL / patent gene-disease annotations (size not verified). Gene and disease dictionaries from `hgnc_protein_coding.parquet` and the 21.04 disease index. **Temporal-clean** if the fiscal-year and filing-date filters hold. Main leakage route: ExPORTER files are regenerated, and abstracts of multi-year projects can be updated after T0; use only project start years <= 2020 and the earliest abstract per project.
- **Minimal test (<= 2 h, CPU).** Download abstracts FY2010-2020; dictionary-match HGNC symbols (case-sensitive, ambiguous symbols dropped) and disease names/synonyms; per (gene, disease) count projects, then pointwise mutual information against the gene and disease marginals; `score = PMI` for pairs with >= 2 projects, blended with transfer by rank. Evaluate `temporal` dev, overall and on non-GWAS arrivals (`fy_clinvar`, `fy_curated`).
- **Expected effect.** Little on GWAS-only arrivals (0), +0.005 to +0.01 nuAP on literature/curated/clinical arrivals, so +0.003 to +0.006 overall; AP +0.003. **Kill rule:** < 0.003 nuAP overall and < 0.008 on the non-GWAS stratum.
- **Most likely failure.** Symbol matching in abstracts is noisy, and the signal that survives is the same as Europe PMC text mining at T0, which is already in the OT literature evidence and therefore in the seeds or the excluded "unknown" set.

## What I would bet on, and what is most underrated

**Bet: WC-L2GEMU.** It attacks the largest hard stratum (57 % of positives, 16 % found; brief 4) with the one signal already shown to have AUC ~0.8, and it adds the missing half: predicting which gene the labeler will pick inside the locus. Its weakness is coverage, which is a data-acquisition problem (more T0 summary statistics), not a modelling one. WC-XPOWER is its extension to diseases without their own study and should be tested in the same session.

**Most underrated: WC-BACKLOG.** It looks like exploiting a benchmark artefact, and that is why nobody will propose it. But up to 40 % of the scored positives are of this kind (brief 5), it needs no shared genes and no seeds at all (so it serves rare diseases with 3-5 seeds), it is cheap, and its result is decisive either way: if it works, every other method's gain must be reported with this arm as a baseline and on the strict endpoint; if it fails, the old-evidence arrivals are not predictable from T0 records and should be removed from the claim.

**A structural recommendation that follows from the lens.** Report every result split by arrival channel (GWAS-only, old-evidence re-attachment, other) with WC-BACKLOG and WC-L2GEMU as channel-specific baselines. A "next-generation" model that only improves the pooled number may be improving the instrument model without anyone noticing.
