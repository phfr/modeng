## Step 1: assumptions of the current best method and baselines

| # | Assumption (transfer LightGBM, kNN transfer, propagation) | If false |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **A zero in the disease x gene matrix means "not associated"** (or at least a uniformly random unlabeled). | Zeros are missing-not-at-random: a pair is zero mostly because nobody had the power or the reason to test it (brief 4: arrivals are "a statistical-power event"). Then relevance and exposure must be modelled separately, and the best candidates are high-relevance, low-exposure pairs (IR-1, IR-4). |
| A2 | **The user is the disease (one row per EFO id).** | The real "sessions" are studies, ClinVar submissions, panels and papers. Rows per EFO id are ontology-propagated duplicates (brief 5) of a much larger, cleaner basket log; item-item similarity should come from baskets (IR-5). |
| A3 | **The item is the gene.** | For 57 % of arrivals (GWAS-only, brief 4) the unit that gets "clicked" is a locus; the gene is chosen from a slate by L2G. Transfer support should be counted per locus and redistributed, which gives support to genes whose neighbour carries the donor label (IR-2). |
| A4 | **The matrix is a static snapshot.** | Associations have dates and an order; diseases are at different stages of their research programme; genes have momentum. Recency-weighted and sequence-aware models then beat a snapshot kNN (IR-6, IR-9). |
| A5 | **One binary behaviour** (overall score above threshold). | There are ~20 evidence channels with a funnel structure (text mining -> sub-threshold GWAS -> animal model -> genetic association -> clinical -> drug). Weak channels of donors are implicit feedback that predicts strong channels later (IR-7). |
| A6 | **Disease identity is stable and correctly resolved at T0.** | ~40 % of "new" positives are old evidence newly attached, plus identifier migration (brief 5). Then part of the task is entity resolution: the evidence is already in the T0 log under another account (IR-3). |
| A7 | **A gene needs interaction history to get a score** (transfer support decides success: 0.4 % vs 69 % top-100, brief 3). | Cold items can be placed in the collaborative latent space from content alone if the model is trained to do exactly that (DropoutNet-style), or they are warm in another interaction log on the same items (IR-8, IR-10). |
| A8 | **Non-association carries no information; all unlabeled genes are equally negative.** | A well-powered study with a flat signal at a gene is an impression without a click: a real negative. Donors' and the target's own nulls should demote candidates, which matters most for nuAP because it cleans hubs' false transfer support (IR-4). |
| A9 | **Popularity is a nuisance to be corrected by degree weights.** | Popularity is two things: true pleiotropy and exposure (research attention). Only the second should be removed; a propensity model separates them where a 1/degree weight cannot (IR-1). |
| A10 | **Pointwise loss on all genes, one global ranking per disease.** | The list is a mixture of arrival mechanisms (curated, GWAS, re-attachment) with different scales; a pointwise GBM lets the dominant mechanism own the top 100. Per-mechanism candidate generators plus a ranker (the standard two-stage retrieval design) would not (design choice used in IR-2/IR-3 tests, not a separate idea). |

## Step 2: ideas

### IR-1 Exposure-aware relevance: model why a pair is unobserved
- **Mechanism.** Factor each cell as P(label) = P(exposed | disease, gene) x P(relevant | disease, gene), as in exposure matrix factorisation, but with exposure *measured* instead of latent: per disease the best GWAS sample size and number of studies at T0, per gene its testability (length, SNP count, LD-block gene density, exome coverage), per pair the research attention of the disease area on the gene. Fit relevance only where exposure is high; rank unobserved pairs by P(relevant) x P(exposure will rise), so high-relevance, under-exposed pairs come first.
- **p_typical** 0.12
- **Data.** `evidence_2104/sourceId=ot_genetics_portal` (`studySampleSize`, `publicationYear` filled, inspected), `gwas_magma_t0` (328 files), `ensembl103_compara`/gene coordinates and `gnomad_v2.1.1` (inventory), 21.04 context matrix. Optional attention prior: NCBI `gene2pubmed.gz` (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/DATA/gene2pubmed.gz, ~200 MB) filtered to PMIDs <= 2021-04. **Leakage class: temporal-clean** if every table is cut at 21.04. Main route: `gene2pubmed` is a current file, so PMIDs must be date-filtered (needs PMID -> year; PubMed baseline or a PMID threshold); current sample sizes of studies must not be used.
- **Minimal test (<= 2 h, CPU).** (1) Build exposure features into `data/cache/expo_t0.parquet` (~20 min). (2) Train on a pseudo-split 19.04 -> 21.04 (`ot_t19_sets` vs `ot_t0_sets`, non-sealed rows): logistic model for exposure-rise, weighted iALS (`implicit` library, 64 factors) for relevance with confidence = exposure. (3) `@register("ir1_expo")` returning `rel * expo_rise`; also `rank-average(ir1_expo, transfer_jaccard)`. Run the temporal dev split (53 diseases), then forward dev. Runtime ~1 h.
- **Expected effect.** Alone below transfer (nuAP ~0.08-0.10). Blended with transfer: +0.010 to +0.020 nuAP, AP +0.000 to +0.005, because the gain is on low-degree GWAS-only positives (16 % top-100 recall today, brief 4) and exposure removes attention-driven hubs. **Kill:** blended nuAP gain over `transfer_jaccard` < +0.008 on temporal dev AND GWAS-only recall@100 gain < 3 points.
- **Most likely failure.** Exposure at T0 is nearly constant within a disease (one big GWAS covers all genes), so the pair-level term collapses into a per-disease scalar that does not change ranks.

### IR-2 Locus slates: transfer per locus, click model per gene
- **Mechanism.** Treat each GWAS locus (LD block) as a slate and the L2G assignment as a click with position bias. Aggregate the donor matrix to disease x locus, run transfer there (a related disease hitting the same block counts even if it was assigned to a *different* gene), then redistribute the locus score to genes with a learned slate-win propensity (how often a gene wins when its block is hit, across all traits, plus distance/coding/constraint features). This gives non-zero support to exactly the genes that have zero gene-level transfer support but sit in a transferred locus.
- **p_typical** 0.05 (locus priors and locus units are on the banned list as "genes near seeds" and as an evaluation unit; locus-level *collaborative* transfer with a click model is neither).
- **Data.** `evidence_2104/sourceId=ot_genetics_portal` (L2G in `resourceScore`, inspected), gene coordinates (`ensembl103_compara` / `ensembl104`, inventory), LD blocks: Berisa & Pickrell EUR blocks (https://bitbucket.org/nygcresearch/ldetect-data, < 1 MB; URL from memory, check), 21.04 context. **Leakage class: temporal-clean.** Main route: win propensities must come from 21.04 L2G only; the ontology family mask must be applied at locus level too (a hidden gene's block is hit in ancestor rows), i.e. zero `fam_rows x block(targets)`, otherwise the parent row reveals the block.
- **Minimal test.** (1) gene -> block map (5 min). (2) `Lb = (L @ G2B > 0)` disease x block; reuse `_transfer` logic on `Lb` with the block-level family mask. (3) win propensity: logistic regression on 21.04 L2G rows (win vs other genes in block), features = rank by TSS distance, coding, pLI, block gene count. (4) `score = transfer_gene + lam * transfer_block[block(g)] * win(g)`, lam chosen on the 19.04 -> 21.04 pseudo-split. `@register("ir2_locus_slate")`, temporal dev + forward dev. Runtime ~40 min CPU.
- **Expected effect.** +0.010 to +0.025 nuAP over transfer, AP +0.003 to +0.008; recall@100 of GWAS-only positives from 16 % (brief 4) to ~22-25 %, top-100 recall of zero-transfer-support positives from 0.4 % (brief 3) to >= 3 %. Why: related traits share loci more often than they share the assigned gene. **Kill:** zero-support top-100 recall < 2 % AND total nuAP gain < +0.008.
- **Most likely failure.** New arrivals are in new loci (power events), not in loci already hit by related diseases, so block-level transfer has the same support hole as gene-level transfer.

### IR-3 Identity linking: retrieve T0 evidence filed under another disease id
- **Mechanism.** About 40 % of "new" positives are old evidence newly attached and identifier migration is sizable (brief 5). So run entity resolution as a retriever: embed the task disease (name, synonyms, ontology cross-references) and every T0 evidence row's free-text `diseaseFromSource` / source id (MedGen CUI, OMIM, Orphanet), and score a gene by the best-matching T0 evidence rows that are mapped to a *different* or no EFO id. Cross-account linking of the same user, done with BM25 plus a dense sentence encoder.
- **p_typical** 0.05 (the label audit is on the banned list as an audit; using the mismatch as a predictor is not).
- **Data.** `evidence_2104/sourceId={eva,eva_somatic,gene2phenotype,genomics_england,clingen,uniprot_variants,uniprot_literature}` (fields 100 % filled for `eva` in the inspected part), `hpo_annotations_2021-01_wayback`, 21.04 ontology with xrefs (harness `data.ontology`). Optional: full ClinVar 2021-04 archive incl. records OT did not ingest (https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pub/clinvar/tab_delimited/archive/2021/variant_summary_2021-04.txt.gz, ~150 MB; path from memory). **Leakage class: temporal-clean** with a string matcher or a pre-2021 encoder (BM25, BioSentVec, SapBERT 2020); **transductive** if a post-2021 LLM embedder is used. Main route: using the 25.09/26.09 ontology xrefs or synonyms to do the matching (that is the future mapping itself); use the 21.04 ontology only.
- **Minimal test.** (1) Collect unique (`diseaseFromSource`, source id, mapped id, gene) tuples from the 7 sources (~15 min, pyarrow column projection). (2) BM25 (`rank_bm25`) + SapBERT cosine between task name/synonyms and source strings; keep rows whose mapped id is not the task or its `fam_rows`. (3) `score(g) = max similarity over rows carrying g`; blend with transfer by rank. `@register("ir3_idlink")`, temporal dev; report separately on arrival class "old evidence" (`groundtruth/arrival_*`) and on strict-a (`alt["sa"]`). Runtime ~45 min, GPU optional (CPU is fine for ~10^5 strings).
- **Expected effect.** Largest single gain on the full temporal endpoint: +0.015 to +0.030 nuAP, AP +0.010 to +0.020 (Mendelian genes are low-degree, so nuAP benefits). ~0 on strict-a and on forward. **Kill:** nuAP gain < +0.010 on temporal dev, or > 80 % of the retrieved hits already fall inside masked family rows (then the harness mask removes them by construction).
- **Most likely failure.** The re-attached evidence sits in the disease's own ontology relatives, which the harness masks (`fam_rows x targets`), so the signal is real but invisible to this benchmark; and it is a curation-pipeline gain, not biology, so it must be reported as its own stratum.

### IR-4 Impressions without clicks: powered nulls as negatives
- **Mechanism.** Recommenders improve sharply when the impression log is available. Build the impression log of human genetics: for each (trait, gene) a well-powered test with a flat result (gene-level z near 0 in a large GWAS) is an exposed negative. Use it three ways: hard negatives in training, a veto on transfer ("similar diseases were shown this gene and did not respond"), and a veto from the target disease's own study.
- **p_typical** 0.07
- **Data.** `modet/data/processed/gwas_magma_t0` (328 files `GCST*_w0/w10`, 4.4 GB, inspected listing), `evidence_2104/sourceId=ot_genetics_portal` for `studySampleSize` and trait -> EFO mapping, `neale_rg_table` (inventory). **Leakage class: temporal-clean** (T0 summary statistics only). Main route: the post-T0 `gwas_magma` directory (38 GB) must not be touched in a temporal run; it is fair only in forward.
- **Minimal test.** (1) Map GCST -> EFO id via `ot_genetics_portal` study ids; per trait compute a power-scaled null score `n(g) = 1[|z_g| < 1] * min(1, N/1e5)`. (2) `score = transfer(g) * (1 - a * n_own(g)) - b * sum_donors sim * n_donor(g)`, a and b tuned on the 19.04 -> 21.04 pseudo-split. (3) `@register("ir4_impressions")`, temporal dev, restricted report on tasks with a mapped T0 GWAS (count them first; if < 15 dev tasks, run on devall-style all non-sealed and say so). Runtime ~30 min CPU.
- **Expected effect.** +0.005 to +0.012 nuAP on tasks with a T0 GWAS (hub false positives demoted), AP +0.000 to +0.004; ~0 elsewhere. **Kill:** < +0.005 nuAP on the GWAS-mapped subset, or recall@100 drops by > 1 point (the veto removes true rare-variant genes that have flat common-variant signal).
- **Most likely failure.** Flat common-variant z is not a negative for Mendelian or somatic genes, and coverage is ~100 traits, so the veto either hurts curated positives or touches too few tasks to pass the MDE.

### IR-5 Study-as-user: item-item similarity from evidence baskets
- **Mechanism.** Replace the 4,592 ontology-propagated disease rows with the raw session log: each GWAS study (GCST), ClinVar submission cluster (RCV grouped by MedGen CUI), gene panel, paper (PMID) and drug programme is a basket of genes. Learn item-item similarity from baskets (session-based item-kNN with inverse basket-frequency and popularity discounting), then score a disease by seed -> item similarity. Baskets are far more numerous and are not duplicated by ontology propagation, so co-association is counted once per independent observation.
- **p_typical** 0.08
- **Data.** `evidence_2104` all sources (4.0 GB; `studyId`, `literature` columns, inspected). **Leakage class: temporal-clean.** Main route: baskets that are the task disease's own future evidence do not exist at T0, but baskets of the task's ontology relatives contain the hidden genes: baskets mapped to `fam_rows` must have the task's targets removed (per-task correction as in `_transfer`), and sealed diseases' baskets dropped entirely.
- **Minimal test.** (1) One pass over evidence -> sparse basket x gene matrix (~10^5-10^6 baskets; ~20 min; cap `europepmc` baskets to PMIDs with 2..50 genes). (2) `S = cosine(B^T B)` with basket weight `1/log(1+|basket|)` and item popularity exponent 0.5; keep top 200 neighbours per gene. (3) `score = S[seeds].sum(0)` with the family correction; `@register("ir5_baskets")`; also blended with `transfer_jaccard`. Temporal dev. Runtime ~1 h CPU, < 20 GB RAM.
- **Expected effect.** Alone ~0.09-0.12 nuAP (comparable to transfer, brief 1); blended +0.008 to +0.015 nuAP, AP +0.005, because paper and panel baskets connect genes that no disease row connects (raises the share of positives with non-zero support). **Kill:** blended gain < +0.008 nuAP, or the share of dev positives with zero support under union(transfer, baskets) falls by < 10 % relative.
- **Most likely failure.** It is the same information as the disease matrix re-cut (item-item EASE on disease rows is already banned as no gain), and text-mined paper baskets import the popularity bias that hurt dense STRING (brief 2).

### IR-6 Trending items: time-decayed transfer on the 19.04 -> 21.04 delta
- **Mechanism.** Standard kNN ignores recency; recommenders weight recent interactions because they predict the next ones. Compute the delta matrix (pairs present at 21.04 but not 19.04), and score with (a) transfer where donor labels are recency-weighted and (b) gene momentum conditioned on the disease neighbourhood: a gene that just spread to several diseases similar to d is likely to reach d next. This is diffusion of a discovery through the ontology and through pleiotropy, read off the release history.
- **p_typical** 0.15
- **Data.** `groundtruth/ot_t19_sets.parquet` (3,435 diseases), `ot_t0_sets.parquet` (4,592), both inspected; optional finer dating from `ot_genetics_portal.publicationYear`. For forward: 21.04, 23.06 (`v2/ot2306_assoc`), 25.09. **Leakage class: temporal-clean.** Main route: id migration between 19.04 and 21.04 makes old pairs look new (brief 5), so the model may learn the curation pipeline; align disease ids through the 21.04 ontology and report strict-a separately.
- **Minimal test.** `L19` re-indexed to the 21.04 rows; `Lw = L21 + beta * (L21 - L19 > 0)`; call the `_transfer` code with `Lw` as the donor matrix (similarity still Jaccard on `L21`); beta in {0.5, 1, 2, 4} chosen on dev by nuAP, plus one fixed pre-registered beta = 1. `@register("ir6_trending")`. Temporal dev. Runtime < 10 min CPU. This is the cheapest test on the list.
- **Expected effect.** +0.005 to +0.015 nuAP over `transfer_jaccard`, AP +0.005; possibly more if ingestion waves continue across releases. **Kill:** < +0.005 nuAP at the pre-registered beta = 1, or the gain disappears on strict-a (then it is pipeline momentum only).
- **Most likely failure.** The 19.04 -> 21.04 delta is dominated by a scoring-model change and source additions in Open Targets, not by discovery order, so "recent" is noise.

### IR-7 Multi-behaviour funnel: weak evidence channels as views, labels as purchases
- **Mechanism.** Build the disease x gene x channel tensor from per-datasource scores (text mining, sub-threshold genetics, animal models, expression, pathways, somatic, clinical, drugs), including scores below the label threshold. Train a multi-behaviour model in which auxiliary channels of the disease and of its neighbours predict the target behaviour (crossing the overall threshold): channel-specific neighbourhoods combined with learned funnel weights. Donor weak evidence is the new part: transfer today only sees donors' binary overall labels.
- **p_typical** 0.20
- **Data.** `/mnt/msadb/modet/downloads/ot2104/associationByDatasourceIndirect` (inspected listing; part of the 679 MB dir). **Leakage class: temporal-clean.** Main route: the harness removes "unknown (weak evidence)" genes from each task's ranking (`tasks.py`), so the task's own weak channels cannot be scored and must also not be used as the model's shortcut; indirect scores are ontology-propagated, so `fam_rows x targets` must be zeroed in every channel.
- **Minimal test.** (1) Load to 20 sparse matrices `C_k` (10 min). (2) Per channel: `T_k = transfer with donor matrix C_k` (similarity from seeds as now). (3) Stack `T_k` + gene degree into a LightGBM lambdarank trained on the 19.04 -> 21.04 pseudo-split or cross-fitted over non-dev temporal tasks, `@register("ir7_funnel")`. Temporal dev. Runtime ~1 h CPU.
- **Expected effect.** +0.008 to +0.015 nuAP vs `transfer_jaccard`; against the modet headline probably +0.000 to +0.008 (its covariates may already absorb part). AP +0.005. **Kill:** < +0.008 nuAP over `transfer_jaccard` on dev.
- **Most likely failure.** Weak channels are dominated by text mining, which follows popularity (dense STRING with text mining hurt, brief 2); the funnel weights then just re-learn degree.

### IR-8 Cold-start item tower trained with interaction dropout
- **Mechanism.** Two-tower model: disease tower (seed-set pooling + ontology position), gene tower = collaborative embedding + content encoder (ESM C, Perturb-seq signatures, DepMap, GTEx, constraint). During training the collaborative part of a gene is dropped at random (DropoutNet), so the content encoder learns to land a gene in the *collaborative latent space* without any interaction history; at inference, genes unknown to any disease get a content-only embedding and compete in the same space. One global model over all ~4.6k diseases, unlike the per-disease ridge heads that were tested.
- **p_typical** 0.35 (new reason despite the banned PoPS-like result: that was a per-disease supervised head scored on all genes; this targets the embedding space, pools supervision over all diseases, and is judged on the cold stratum, where within-pool AUC is 0.60-0.63, brief 3).
- **Data.** `modet/data/processed/features/` (ESM C 300M, GO T0, GTEx v8, Reactome T0), subex derived matrices (DepMap 20Q4, Replogle K562/RPE1 signatures) per inventory. **Leakage class: temporal-clean** with T0 features (DepMap 20Q4, GTEx v8, Replogle is 2022 -> **transductive** if included; CD4 2025 / Nadig 2025 only in forward). Main route: post-T0 perturbation atlases and current GO.
- **Minimal test.** PyTorch, 128-d towers, sampled softmax with log-popularity correction, dropout rate 0.5 on the collaborative embedding, train on the 21.04 context minus dev tasks' family pairs; `gpu-run --mem 6 -- python -m modeng.methods.ir8_train` (~20 min) or CPU (~1 h). `@register("ir8_coldtower")`; evaluate overall and on the stratum "target genes with no disease at T0" (`groundtruth/arrival_t0_known_genes.parquet`). Do not start while other sealed GPU runs are queued (`gpu-run status`).
- **Expected effect.** Overall nuAP +0.000 to +0.008 when blended; cold-stratum within-pool AUC from ~0.62 to 0.68-0.70. **Kill:** cold-stratum AUC < 0.66 on temporal dev AND overall gain < +0.005.
- **Most likely failure.** Content features do not carry disease-specific signal (brief 2: 57k features added +0.000-0.007); the tower learns a generic "disease-gene-likeness" prior, which the tier-0 propensity prior already covers.

### IR-9 Next-item model over dated discovery sequences
- **Mechanism.** Order each disease's genes by first-evidence date (GWAS publication year, PMID year of curated and text-mined evidence) and train a self-attentive next-item model (SASRec-type) across all diseases; the query is the disease's history, the output a distribution over the next genes. It can learn stage effects that a set-based kNN cannot: e.g. after a run of GWAS loci in one pathway come its drug targets, and after the first Mendelian genes of a complex come the other subunits. Item embeddings initialised from content so cold genes are scorable.
- **p_typical** 0.20
- **Data.** `evidence_2104` (`publicationYear` filled for `ot_genetics_portal`; `literature` PMIDs for `europepmc` and others, inspected; PMID -> year needs the PubMed baseline or NCBI `PMC-ids`/eutils batch, ~1 GB, or a monotone PMID-to-year approximation). **Leakage class: temporal-clean.** Main route: dates of first evidence taken from a later release (use 21.04 evidence only); sequences of ontology relatives contain the task's hidden genes only after T0, so no extra mask is needed beyond dropping sealed rows.
- **Minimal test.** (1) First-date table per (disease, gene) (~30 min). (2) SASRec, 2 layers, d = 64, max length 200, trained on prefixes ending <= 2019, validated on 2019-2021 arrivals; `gpu-run --mem 4 -- python -m modeng.methods.ir9_train` (~20 min). (3) `@register("ir9_nextitem")`: score = next-item logits given the full T0 sequence; compare with the same model on shuffled order (the claim-deciding ablation: if shuffled = ordered, order carries nothing). Temporal dev.
- **Expected effect.** Alone ~0.10 nuAP; ordered minus shuffled +0.003 to +0.010; blended with transfer +0.005. **Kill:** ordered minus shuffled < +0.005 nuAP.
- **Most likely failure.** Median ~11 seeds (brief 6) gives sequences too short for order to matter, and dates are coarse (years) and missing for curated sources.

### IR-10 Cross-domain users: genes cold for diseases are warm in burden PheWAS
- **Mechanism.** Add a second interaction log over the same items: exome gene-burden PheWAS (every protein-coding gene tested against thousands of phenotypes, sub-threshold statistics included) and mouse knockout phenotypes. Learn one shared gene space by collective factorisation of disease x gene (binary) and phenotype x gene (burden z, confidence-weighted), with phenotypes linked to diseases by ontology / text. Genes without any disease association have rows of real signal in the burden log, which is how cross-domain recommendation handles cold items.
- **p_typical** 0.15
- **Data.** Genebass gene-level burden results (https://app.genebass.org/downloads, Hail tables on public cloud buckets; gene-level tables are tens of GB, size not checked) or AstraZeneca PheWAS portal gene-level downloads (https://azphewas.com, availability of bulk files not checked); `impc_release-13.0` on disk (inventory). **Leakage class: transductive on temporal** (Genebass and AZ PheWAS were released 2021-2022, after T0); **clean on forward** (inputs <= 2025-09) and usable on holdout. Main route on forward: Open Targets ingests significant burden results as evidence, so 26.09 targets may be direct thresholded copies of the input; exclude pairs with p < 1e-4 in the burden input for the task's mapped phenotypes and report with and without.
- **Minimal test.** Forward dev only. (1) Download gene x phenotype burden z (pLoF and missense|LC masks), keep phenotypes with >= 5k cases (~40 min incl. download). (2) Phenotype neighbours of each task disease by Jaccard between seeds and the phenotype's top-100 burden genes; `score = sum sim * z_burden` (the transfer code with a real-valued donor matrix). (3) `@register("ir10_burden_xdomain", benches=("forward","holdout"))`; blend with `transfer_jaccard`. Runtime ~1.5 h CPU.
- **Expected effect.** Forward: +0.005 to +0.015 nuAP blended, concentrated on genes with no disease at 25.09 (within-pool AUC +0.03-0.05). AP ~0. **Kill:** < +0.005 nuAP on forward dev after removing near-significant own-phenotype pairs.
- **Most likely failure.** UK Biobank burden tests are underpowered for most genes, so the matrix is noise outside ~500 genes that are already known disease genes.

## Bets

- **Would bet on: IR-2 (locus slates).** It is the only idea here that manufactures transfer support for the stratum that today has none (0.4 % top-100, brief 3) from data already on disk, it attacks the largest arrival class (57 % GWAS-only, brief 4), it is temporal-clean, and its kill rule is decided in under an hour. IR-6 should be run first anyway because it costs ten minutes.
- **Most underrated: IR-3 (identity linking).** It looks like bookkeeping, but brief 5 says the re-attachment mass is ~40 % of the temporal positives, larger than any modelling gain on the list. Its value depends on one check that should be made before anything else: what fraction of re-attached positives lies outside the masked family rows. If most are inside, the idea is dead on this harness and belongs in the label definition instead.
- Cross-cutting observation from the lens: IR-1, IR-4 and IR-2 are three parts of one model (exposure, exposed negatives, slate position bias). If two of the three pass their kill rules, the round-2 proposal should be a single click-model formulation of gene discovery, not three features.
